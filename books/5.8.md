## 5.8 What We Learned

The whole arc of this chapter comes down to one recurring question: **how do you give a model access to the entire context at once, instead of piece by piece?**

RNNs tried to solve this sequentially and ran into:

- Lost long-range connections
- The impossibility of parallelism

The transformer dropped sequence entirely. Here's the arc:

1. Every token becomes three projections — **Q, K, and V**
2. It compares its own query against every key in the sentence
3. That comparison produces a set of importance weights
4. Those weights assemble a new, context-enriched version of the token out of everyone else's V

And to avoid relying on just one way of looking at the sentence, this whole process **runs in parallel across several attention heads.**

> None of it is hand-designed — the entire structure is learned by the model as a side effect of one simple goal: predicting the next token as accurately as possible.
