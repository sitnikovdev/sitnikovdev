# 5.8 What We Learned

The whole arc of this chapter comes down to one recurring question: how do you give a model access to the entire context at once, instead of piece by piece? RNNs tried to solve this sequentially and ran into lost long-range connections and the impossibility of parallelism. The transformer dropped sequence entirely: every token becomes three projections — Q, K, and V — compares its own query against every key in the sentence, gets a set of importance weights, and assembles a new, context-enriched version of itself out of everyone else's V. And to avoid relying on just one way of looking at the sentence, this whole process runs in parallel across several attention heads. None of it is hand-designed — the entire structure is learned by the model as a side effect of one simple goal: predicting the next token as accurately as possible.
