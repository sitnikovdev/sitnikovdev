## 5.7 One Head Isn't Enough: Multi-Head Attention

A single token can have several different kinds of relationships to the words around it — grammatical, semantic, thematic — and one set of Q/K/V simply can't capture all of them equally well.

So instead of one triple of matrices, the model sets up **several independent triples at once** — attention "heads" — each computing its own Q, K, V, its own weights, and its own result, in parallel with the rest.

Because each head starts from its own random initialization, during training they naturally drift toward **different specializations** that help with accurate prediction.

The results from all the heads are then:

1. Concatenated into one vector
2. Passed through yet another trainable matrix, which blends them into the token's final representation
